# Ten questions in nonlinear deep learning theory

Wentao Li · Prepared with assistance from OpenAI Codex · November 21, 2026

A graph transformer can use the electrical resistance between every pair of nodes and still miss whether the graph is bipartite. Another draft finds the intrinsic-dimension exponent governing the best possible spherical cross-entropy loss. The remaining papers study what happens when noisy attention starts to cluster, when training a random sparse network can fail to attain its optimum, and what graph and set architectures can represent.

Each manuscript has a proposed proof or disproof, the original question and its authors, and a statement of scope. The scores estimate the significance of the result if correct and new. The Riemann hypothesis and three-dimensional Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness anchor the scale at 100. Proofs await independent review. Priority was checked against the cited literature and remains provisional. All ten Lean entries are open.

## Proof and priority notes

- **Resistance and shortest paths.** Zhang, Luo, Wang and He established one direction of separation. Black and coauthors later reported experimental evidence for the reverse. The signed-lift construction here establishes the reverse direction for any number of refinement rounds, and gives different adjacency and Laplacian spectra. In the smallest construction in the note, one 78-vertex graph is bipartite and the other contains two triangles. The accompanying exact calculation compares every resistance pair within corresponding fibers. [ICLR paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09505) · [ICML comparison](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/black24b.html)
- **Spherical cross-entropy.** Lu and Steinerberger proved uniqueness of the continuum minimizer and used spherical designs for an upper bound. The new argument finds a polynomial missed by every finite configuration and transfers its quadrature error through the logarithm in the loss. It contradicts the rate printed in Theorem 5 of their arXiv v2 above intrinsic dimension one. The exponent is sharp up to constants in the logarithm of the gap. [Original statement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08465v2) · [Sphere-design theorem](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2013/178-2/p02)
- **Noisy attention.** Mun and Rosenzweig explicitly predicted two asymptotic laws for the exponential kernel and described their verification as ongoing work. The large-inverse-temperature argument derives the additive constant and Gaussian profile. The tricritical argument uses a sixth-order expansion and a separate global minimizer argument. The counterexample to uniqueness uses the more general kernel class in their Remark 2.4. Uniqueness for the exponential kernel in that interval is still open here. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05140) · [Earlier work on the circle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20094)
- **Full and sampled deletion decks.** The full-deck Lanczos conjecture appeared in the early public versions. The later AAAI paper implements sampled constraints. The null-space theorem concerns the simultaneous full deck, whose degree-weighted constraint matrix has exactly one surviving direction per bipartite component. [Original arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12334v1) · [AAAI version](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/39044)
- **Random sparse training.** The data can depend on the mask in the original question. The nine-point construction exploits that quantifier. A separate Poisson law gives the probability for the linear matrix-product test. Neither probability measures test-set error or the frequency of failure on randomly drawn examples. [NeurIPS question](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02666)
