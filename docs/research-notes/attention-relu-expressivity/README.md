# Ten questions about graph transformers, attention and ReLU networks

Wentao Li · Prepared with assistance from OpenAI Codex · November 21, 2026

Can a graph transformer count cycles if it sees both shortest paths and electrical resistance? Can finitely many Gaussian moments distinguish every pair of functions in a fixed ReLU architecture? The first two manuscripts answer these questions for all finite graph patterns and all network weights, respectively.

Six catalogue drafts give proposed answers to specific questions in the cited papers. Four companion notes prove results under additional hypotheses from broader research programs. Scores estimate the significance of each stated result if correct and new, on a scale with 3D Navier–Stokes and the Riemann hypothesis at 100. The literature comparisons are provisional. Independent proof review and Lean formalization are pending for all ten.

## What was checked

- **Graph counts.** Shortest paths alone were treated in an earlier catalogue draft. Grounding the hub of the new construction shifts the Laplacian by the identity. The replacement paths cancel at this shift while preserving the association of adjacency with resistance. The included exact calculation compares all paired labels across two 385-vertex examples. The proof constructs a witness for every cyclic pattern. [Earlier shortest-path result](../../../results/learning-theory/shortest-path-homomorphisms/README.md) · [New proof and verification script](../../../results/learning-theory/joint-distance-forest-counts/README.md)
- **Finite measurements.** The Gaussian argument reduces a directional transform to a differential system on the faces of polyhedra. A multiplicity estimate bounds how many leading moments can vanish. Classical inverse-moment results and one-dimensional moment-vanishing theory are cited in the manuscript. The measurements are population expectations and their degree bound can be very large. [Proof and sources](../../../results/learning-theory/global-relu-moments/README.md)
- **Loss normalization.** Equation (2) of the COLT paper gives radial second derivative 1 for a single matched unit, while the printed Hessian formula (5) gives 1/2. On an explicit two-teacher path, differentiating the exact Gaussian kernel loss gives the strict inequality in the opposite direction for the second convention. The manuscript supplies a general neighborhood proof for the first convention. [COLT paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v134/safran21a.html) · [Proof and reproducible check](../../../results/learning-theory/relu-curvature-normalization/README.md)
- **Attention and moving tokens.** The independence criterion uses independent uniform initialization and includes discontinuous coexistence. The curvature example uses the intrinsic metric on fixed-mass configurations. Extending it to transport curves that split mass is open. The collapse rate concerns a dissipative matrix class. [Independence proof](../uniform-time-noisy-attention/README.md) · [Curvature proof and check](../twisted-attention-curvature/README.md) · [Collapse proof](../dissipative-attention-rates/README.md)
