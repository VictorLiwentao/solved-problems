## What warrants review first

**Choice and rainbow colorings.** The model for row 1 uses a class of finite structures with a partial function. Its freely ordered version has the structural Ramsey property by an existing theorem, so its automorphism group is extremely amenable. This supplies the Boolean prime ideal theorem while the partial function gives a bounded coloring that collides in every infinite symmetric subset. The transfer to pure ZF uses Pincus's theorem. Row 2 uses symmetry among the petals of an unordered configuration. Row 3 pairs two sets with an intersection of prescribed size. In the same manuscript, the lower-arity property can hold while a higher-arity property fails, as requested in Q8.3. The converse comparisons in Q7.16 remain open in this argument and are outside the ten answers. [Manuscript](../../../results/set-theory/rainbow-ramsey-choice-models/README.md) · [Structural Ramsey theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02379)

**Relative density.** The proof of row 4 has two steps worth checking separately. Ties at the largest random weight give a positive probability of each prescribed target under a limiting law. A second forcing realizes the resulting conditional probabilities by a permutation even on sparse infinite denominators. The claim is a relative consistency result. The lower bound against countable families holds in ZFC. [Manuscript](../../../results/set-theory/two-target-relative-density/README.md)

The two verification scripts check closures and orbits in finite rainbow configurations, and the exact probability of a tie on finite samples. The models, forcing iteration, transfer and measure-space arguments are written proofs. There are no Lean formalizations in this batch.
