# When Tukey-top ultrafilters exist generically

Wentao Li · Research draft with OpenAI Codex assistance · November 22, 2026

Cancino-Manríquez and Zapletal ask for a characterization of generic existence of Tukey-top ultrafilters on $\omega$. In the small-filter formulation used just after their Question 11.6, the exact criterion is $\mathfrak u=\mathfrak c$. There is also a local criterion for filters of arbitrary character. [Original preprint, question added July 2025 as Q11.4 in v2, Q11.6 in v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08699)

**Definitions.** All filters here are proper, free and contain the cofinite sets. The character $\chi(F)$ is the least size of a base under inclusion, $\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}$, and $\mathfrak u$ is the least character among free ultrafilters. Tukey order compares directed sets by maps preserving cofinal subsets. An ultrafilter is directed by reverse inclusion. Tukey-top means having cofinal type $([\mathfrak c]^{<\omega},\subseteq)$. A class exists generically if every free filter of character less than $\mathfrak c$ extends to a member. This concerns extensions in the given universe.

**Extension lemma (Horvath and Özalp, Fact 4.11).** If $F$ has infinitely many ultrafilter extensions, then for every free ultrafilter $V$ there is an extension $W$ of $F$ such that $V\le_{\rm RK} W$. To see this, choose pairwise disjoint sets $B_n\subseteq\omega$ and extensions $U_n$ with $B_n\in U_n$. Here is a direct construction. Start with all extensions in the cell $\omega$. An infinite collection in a cell contains two extensions distinguished by some set $A$. Split the cell using $A$ and its complement. Both new cells have extensions and at least one has infinitely many. Continue in the latter cell and retain the other as $B_n$, choosing one of its extensions $U_n$.

Put $W=\{A\subseteq\omega:\{n:A\in U_n\}\in V\}$. This is an ultrafilter by the Boolean laws, is free, and contains $F$. Define $f:\omega\to\omega$ to equal $n$ on $B_n$ and assign any value outside their union. For each $D\subseteq\omega$, $f^{-1}[D]\in U_n$ exactly when $n\in D$, since $B_n\in U_n$. Thus $f_*(W)=V$. In particular $V\le_T W$: the monotone map $A\mapsto f[A]$ on $W$ has cofinal image in $V$. Choose for $V$ a Tukey-top ultrafilter, whose existence is classical by Isbell. Every directed set of size at most $\mathfrak c$ lies Tukey below $[\mathfrak c]^{<\omega}$, so $W$ is top.

If $F$ instead has only finitely many extensions, each one has the form of the filter generated by $F$ and one additional set. Indeed isolate that extension $U$ from the other finitely many by a set $B\in U$ (take a finite intersection of separating sets). The filter generated by $F\cup\{B\}$ has $U$ as its unique ultrafilter extension, hence equals $U$ by the ultrafilter lemma. Consequently each extension has character at most $\max(\aleph_0,\chi(F))$. For arbitrary $F$, the two alternatives give the local criterion: infinitely many extensions always suffice, and in the finite case one of the finitely many extensions must itself be top.

**Generic existence.** If $\mathfrak u=\mathfrak c$ and $\chi(F)<\mathfrak c$, the finite-extension case is impossible, by the character bound. The extension lemma supplies a Tukey-top ultrafilter above $F$. If $\mathfrak u<\mathfrak c$, take a free ultrafilter of character $\mathfrak u$. Its only extension is itself, and its cofinality is less than that of $[\mathfrak c]^{<\omega}$, which is $\mathfrak c$. It is not Tukey-top. Even under a convention that tests only filters which are not ultrafilters, take two isomorphic copies of this ultrafilter on disjoint halves of $\omega$ and intersect their lifts. The resulting filter has a base of size $\mathfrak u$ and its only two extensions are the original lifts: an ultrafilter chooses one of the two halves, where its trace is already determined.

**Scope and priority.** Ultrafilter sums, the Rudin–Keisler projection and Isbell's existence theorem are classical. Horvath and Özalp explicitly prove the extension lemma in Fact 4.11 and cite Banakh and Blass for the character bound in their Proposition 2.9. Proposition 4.12 already gives Tukey-top points in every infinite closed set of free ultrafilters. Applying their results gives the equivalence with $\mathfrak u=\mathfrak c$ and the filter-by-filter argument. The stronger statement mentioned in the source about adjoining just $\aleph_1$ sets to *force every extension* to be Tukey-top is a different assertion. This proof selects one extension.
