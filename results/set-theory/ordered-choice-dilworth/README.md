# Ordered choice, Dilworth’s theorem and the Erdős–Dushnik–Miller principle

Wentao Li · Prepared with assistance from OpenAI GPT-6.2 Astra

[Proof (PDF)](ordered-choice-dilworth.pdf) · [LaTeX source](ordered-choice-dilworth.tex) · [Finite verification](verify.py) · [Download BibTeX](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VictorLiwentao/solved-problems/main/results/set-theory/ordered-choice-dilworth/citation.bib)

Two published questions ask what follows from choice for families indexed by a linearly ordered set in set theory with atoms. This draft proves Dilworth’s theorem from that assumption and gives a model where the Erdős–Dushnik–Miller principle fails even with dependent choice. [Dilworth question, §6.5](https://dml.cz/handle/10338.dmlcz/151857) · [Partition question, §6.4](https://doi.org/10.4064/ba221221-6-6)

## Why the two conclusions differ

In a finite-width poset, the connected components of the incomparability graph form a linear order. Within one component, assign each vertex at the next graph distance to its first neighbor in the previous layer under a well-order of that layer. Each part has smaller width. This gives a well-order of the whole poset, where a transfinite greedy coloring proves Dilworth’s theorem. The argument extends by ordinal induction to a well-founded tree of finite antichains.

For the graph statement, take uncountably many interchangeable, unlabelled pairs as atoms. In the model with countable supports any independent set of the matching lies within its own countable support. Yet ordered choice holds. The permutations fixing a support are generated by involutions and act trivially on every linear order they preserve. A single countable reserve of pairs then suffices to support simultaneous choices.

## Proof and priority status

The well-orderable-vertex case of Dilworth and several weaker-choice separations were known. The rank argument addresses the Dilworth question. The matching separation in ZFA and the model used here were published by Tachtsis in 2024 and are described as the model Nₜ by Howard and Tachtsis in 2026. [Tachtsis, Theorem 3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00605-023-01933-z) · [Howard and Tachtsis, §6.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18229) In pure ZF ordered choice already implies full choice. The checker exhausts all 4,231 labelled five-point posets and 3,840 permutations of five pairs. The infinite induction and model argument are in the manuscript. Independent proof and priority review and Lean formalization are pending. [Tachtsis’s original study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10474-019-00967-w)
