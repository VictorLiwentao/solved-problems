# No tall summable ideal is Katětov-homogeneous

Wentao Li · Research draft with OpenAI Codex assistance · November 22, 2026

[Proof (PDF)](summable-restrictions.pdf) · [LaTeX source](summable-restrictions.tex) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Filipów, Kowitz and Kwela ask whether the harmonic summable ideal is Katětov-homogeneous (Question 8.8(2)) and whether its Bolzano–Weierstrass property implies the hereditary version (Question 10.4(1a)). The first answer is negative for every tall summable ideal. Under CH the second answer is negative for every such ideal as well. [Original questions and comparison theorem, first posted 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/jsl.2024.8)

**Definitions.** Let $w_i>0$, $\sum_i w_i=\infty$ and $w_i\to0$. The tall summable ideal $I_w$ consists of subsets of indices of finite total weight. Reorder the weights nonincreasingly, which preserves the ideal up to isomorphism. Denote their partial sums by $W(n)=\sum_{i<n}w_i$. For a positive subset $A$, list its weights nonincreasingly as $v_0,v_1,\ldots$, and put $V(n)=\sum_{i<n}v_i$. Homogeneity in the question would require $I_w\restriction A\le_K I_w$ for every positive $A$.

**Mass bound for any reduction.** For arbitrary nonincreasing tall summable weights $w,v$, if $I_v\le_K I_w$ then $W(n)\le C V(n)+K$ for all $n$, where $C,K$ are finite constants. Indeed let $f$ witness the reduction, and set $u_j=\sum_{f(i)=j}w_i$. Fibers have finite weight since singletons belong to $I_v$. Also $u_j\to0$: otherwise select infinitely many with $u_j$ bounded below and total $v$-weight finite, using $v_j\to0$. The reduction says $\sum_{j\in E}v_j<\infty$ implies $\sum_{j\in E}u_j<\infty$.

There is some $C$ for which the exceptional set $\{j:u_j>Cv_j\}$ has finite $u$-mass $K$. Otherwise for each $m\ge1$ successively take disjoint finite blocks of fresh exceptional indices with $u_j>2^m v_j$ and total $u$-mass in $[1,2]$. These exist since the exceptional mass is infinite and $u_j\le1$ eventually. Their union contradicts the reduction. Now the image of the first $n$ domain points has size at most $n$. Its $u$-mass is at least $W(n)$ and at most $K+C V(n)$ by monotonicity of $v$. This proves the necessary bound.

**A positive restriction violating the bound.** Choose strictly increasing $N_j$ with $W(N_j)\ge j^2$. Successively choose finite blocks of indices, each entirely beyond the preceding block. In block $j\ge1$, use weights at most $1/N_{j+1}$ and stop when the mass first reaches one. Every tail has infinite mass, so this is possible. Each block has mass at most two and contains at least $N_{j+1}$ elements. The union $A$ has infinite mass. If a prefix of its nonincreasing enumeration ends in block $j\ge2$, its length $n$ is at least $N_j$ and its mass at most $2j$. Consequently $V(n)/W(n)\to0$. The mass bound forbids a Katětov reduction $I_w\restriction A\le_K I_w$. This proves the ZFC theorem.

For harmonic weights, one can also see the obstruction at the familiar logarithmic scales. The increasing indices $a_i=\lceil i\log(i+1)\rceil$ for $i\ge2$ give a divergent restriction with partial sums of order $\log\log n$, whereas the original partial sums grow as $\log n$.

**Spaces.** Write $\mathrm{FinBW}(I)$ for the spaces where every sequence indexed by the ideal's domain has a converging restriction on an $I$-positive set. Its hereditary version asks the same for every sequence given on any positive subset of the domain. Both $I_w$ and $I_w\restriction A$ are $F_\sigma$ and $P^+$. For the latter property, given decreasing positive sets, take one disjoint finite block of mass at least one from each successive set. Their union has infinite mass and is almost contained in every original set. Apply the CH comparison theorem of Filipów, Kowitz and Kwela (Theorem 16.1(2b), with their ideal partition function for $I_w\restriction A$). It gives a compact separable Hausdorff space in $\mathrm{FinBW}(I_w)$ outside $\mathrm{FinBW}(I_w\restriction A)$. Proposition 10.2(1b) then excludes the space from the hereditary class. This is a consistency counterexample to the topological equality. It does not decide their separate finite-product question.

**Priority.** General comparisons between summable ideals go back to Meza-Alcántara and Flašková. Kowitz and Tryba's 2025 theorem on Question 8.8(1) treats the difference function.
