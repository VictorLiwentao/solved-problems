# One family suffices for all interior densities

Wentao Li · Research draft with OpenAI Codex assistance · November 22, 2026

[Proof (PDF)](interior-density.pdf) · [LaTeX source](interior-density.tex) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Valderrama proved that the density-disruption number for one interior density is independent of the chosen density and extended the equality to sets of at most $\aleph_1$ densities. Question 4.2 asks if allowing the whole interval can consistently increase that cardinal or its relative-density reaping analogue. Both equalities hold in ZFC. [Valderrama, 2025, Theorem 2.9, Corollary 2.10 and Question 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21059) · [Brech, Brendle and Telles, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21102) · [Farkas, Klausner and Lischka, §5](https://doi.org/10.1017/jsl.2023.62)

**Statement.** For a nonempty $X\subseteq(0,1)$, let $\mathfrak r_X$ be the least size of a family of infinite sets $R$ such that for every $p\in X$ and infinite coinfinite $S$, at least one relative density $d_R(S)$ either fails to exist or differs from $p$. Let $\mathfrak{dd}_{X,\mathrm{all}}$ be the least size of a permutation family changing the density of each set whose density is in $X$, with oscillation an allowed output. Then
$$
 \mathfrak r_X=\mathfrak r_{\{1/2\}},\qquad
 \mathfrak{dd}_{X,\mathrm{all}}=\mathfrak{dd}_{\{1/2\},\mathrm{all}} .
$$
It suffices to prove the equalities for $X=(0,1)$. Monotonicity and the known equalities for all singletons then sandwich the other cases.

**Building independent splits.** Suppose $\mathcal R$ has cardinality less than $\mathfrak r_{(0,1)}$. Construct $(S_\alpha,p_\alpha)_{\alpha<\omega_1}$ with $0<p_\alpha<1$. For each $R\in\mathcal R$ every finite pattern of membership in the $S_\alpha$ will have relative density in $R$ equal to its Bernoulli product probability with parameters $p_\alpha$.

Add $\omega$ to the initial family. At stage $\alpha$ put into a test family every intersection of an initial $R$ with a finite Boolean cell of the earlier $S_\beta$, using the full set as the empty cell. The induction hypothesis gives each such intersection strictly positive relative density in $R$, hence infinitely many elements. There are at most $\max(|\mathcal R|,\aleph_0)$ tests, still below $\mathfrak r_{(0,1)}$: countably many infinite sets can all be split at density $1/2$ by a single fair-coin set, by the strong law. By the definition of the cardinal there exist **one** $S_\alpha$ and **one** $p_\alpha\in(0,1)$ for which the relative density equals $p_\alpha$ in every test. The elementary identity $d_R(C\cap S)=d_R(C)d_{R\cap C}(S)$ when the two limits exist and $d_R(C)>0$ proves the induction step. At limit stages only finite patterns have to be retained.

**Extracting a fair split.** The interval $(0,1)$ is the union of the closed strips $[1/m,1-1/m]$ for integers $m\ge2$. One strip contains infinitely many of the $\omega_1$ parameters (in fact uncountably many). Choose distinct indices $\alpha_j$ in it. The product probability $\mu$ for the selected bits assigns mass at most $(1-1/m)^k$ to every length-$k$ cylinder. Map a bit string to its usual binary real in $[0,1]$. Each real has at most two preimages, so the pushed-forward distribution has no atoms and has a continuous distribution function. For any $0<q<1$, choose a threshold $t$ whose lower interval has mass $q$. Let $T$ consist of the integers whose selected membership bits encode a real less than $t$. Finite unions of length-$k$ cylinders bracket this event with mass difference at most $2(1-1/m)^k$ (the factor two allows both binary expansions). Each such finite union has exactly its prescribed relative density along each $R$. Squeezing proves $d_R(T)=q$ for all $R$. Taking $q=1/2$ shows that every family smaller than $\mathfrak r_{(0,1)}$ fails to witness $\mathfrak r_{\{1/2\}}$. The opposite inequality is monotonicity.

**Permutation version.** Start with a family $\Pi$ smaller than $\mathfrak{dd}_{(0,1),\mathrm{all}}$ and add the identity. The inductive requirement is now that, for every $\pi\in\Pi$, every finite cell $C$ has $d(\pi[C])$ equal to its product probability. Given an infinite $D$, fix a permutation $\theta_D$ that maps $D$ increasingly onto a set of density one. If the complement of $D$ is finite, use a complement of the same size. Otherwise use an infinite density-zero complement. Then $d(\theta_D[Z])=d_D(Z)$ whenever either limit exists, with existence equivalent. This is the coding in Valderrama's Lemma 2.2.

At a countable stage test all composed permutations $\theta_{\pi[C]}\circ\pi$ for old finite cells $C$ and $\pi\in\Pi$. Their number is still below the cardinal: countably many permutations preserve density $1/2$ for a common fair-coin set almost surely. Hence there is a set $S_\alpha$ of some ordinary density $p_\alpha\in(0,1)$ with that same density under all test permutations. Consequently $\pi[S_\alpha]$ splits each $\pi[C]$ with relative density $p_\alpha$. Multiplication gives the required frequencies at the next stage. Repeat through $\omega_1$ and use the same threshold construction. Permutations preserve the Boolean operations and the membership-bit description of $T$, so $d(\pi[T])=q$ for every $\pi\in\Pi$, including the identity. Taking $q=1/2$ gives the reverse cardinal inequality as before.

**Scope and priority.** Fixed biases, countable unions of bias requirements, and the permutation coding have precedents in the sources. The transfinite construction deals with biases chosen by successive witnesses and gives a single atomless distribution on the extracted bits for every original test. The theorem treats arbitrary nonempty input sets of interior densities with unrestricted output types. The dual permutation cardinals and restricted output types impose different requirements. Provisional significance **62/100** if correct and new. Independent proof and priority review are pending.
