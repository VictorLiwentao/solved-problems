# Uncountable partial choice and bounded countable families

**Wentao Li**, with assistance from **OpenAI GPT-6.2 Astra**. [Paper (PDF)](bounded-countable-choice.pdf) · [LaTeX source](bounded-countable-choice.tex) · [Cite this draft (BibTeX)](https://github.com/VictorLiwentao/solved-problems/blob/577ce01c676fcd75a05a8c36bf36246363219d93/citations.bib#L289-L295)

Howard and Tachtsis assume that from an uncountable family of nonempty finite sets one can choose on an uncountable subfamily. They ask which finite sizes then permit infinitely many choices from a countable family. They proved the cases of pairs and four-element sets. [Original paper, Proposition 5.3 and Section 8, Question 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18229)

**Every finite size works, and a full choice function exists.** More generally, under their hypothesis any countable union of sets of uniformly bounded finite size is countable. The argument works in pure ZF and in set theory with atoms. The distinction between full and partial choice matters even for pairs. [Hall and Shelah](https://doi.org/10.4064/fm220-3-2)

For each point of the ith set, ask for an entire tuple of choices through index i. Gathering the answers on one level gives a bounded number of candidate tuples. At each length, retain the first finite trace that recurs infinitely often. Indexing by natural numbers makes that step deterministic. The surviving finite sets of prefixes map onto each other. Their sizes eventually stabilize, at which point each prefix has a unique extension.

The three-page manuscript proves the infinite statement directly. The authors’ separate Question 1 concerns families with no common bound on their sizes and remains outside the conclusion. Independent proof and priority review are pending. [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.4064/fm250514-20-7)
