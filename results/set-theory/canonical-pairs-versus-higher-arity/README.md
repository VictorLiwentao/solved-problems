# Canonical pairs and higher-arity Ramsey finiteness

Wentao Li · Research draft with OpenAI Codex assistance · November 22, 2026

[Proof (PDF)](canonical-pairs-versus-higher-arity.pdf) · [LaTeX source](canonical-pairs-versus-higher-arity.tex) · [BibTeX](citation.bib)

Venkataramani's Question 8.1 asks how the failure of the canonical Ramsey theorem for pairs compares with the classes $R_n$-Fin of Brot, Cao and Fernández-Bretón. There are two indices to distinguish. In the latter authors' definition $n$ is the size of a colored subset. In the proof of Venkataramani's Theorem 5.3, $n$ is the number of colors of **pairs**. We answer the arity formulation by canonizing pair colorings in the ordered random triple system. [Venkataramani, 2026, Definition 4.3, Theorem 5.3 and Question 8.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22196) · [Brot, Cao and Fernández-Bretón, Definition 2.1 and Proposition 4.25](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11025)

**Statement.** In ZF, $R_2$-Fin is contained in CRT-Fin, and this inclusion is consistently strict. In fact a single model witnesses strict inclusion for all finite arities. For every $n\ge3$ neither inclusion between $R_n$-Fin and CRT-Fin holds in all models of ZF. Here CRT$(X)$ requires an infinite linearly ordered subset on which a given pair coloring is constant, injective, or determined exactly by its first or its last coordinate. A set is $R_n$-finite if some binary coloring of its $n$-element subsets has no infinite homogeneous set.

**The known direction.** For a finite coloring of pairs, the two one-coordinate canonical forms and the injective form would require infinitely many colors on an infinite linearly ordered set. CRT$(X)$ therefore implies pair homogeneity for every finite palette, as Venkataramani showed. In the basic Fraenkel model of atoms with finite supports and full symmetric group, every finitely supported finite coloring of $n$-sets with pure color labels is constant off the support, by transitivity. No infinite subset of the atoms is linearly orderable there. A transposition outside supports for the subset and the proposed order exchanges two of its members while preserving the order, a contradiction. Thus CRT fails on a set satisfying every finite-color, finite-arity Ramsey property. This example and the pair implication already follow from the cited literature.

To address the source's separate consistency quantifier, strict inclusion $R_n$-Fin $\subsetneq$ CRT-Fin can hold simultaneously for every finite $n\ge2$. In the basic Fraenkel model, every non-well-orderable set contains a copy of an infinite subset of the atoms, by Blass's lemma. Every infinite subset of the atoms is cofinite, so the preceding transitivity argument applies to any of its finite colorings. The infinite well-orderable sets satisfy ordinary Ramsey by restricting to a countable subset. Hence global finite-arity Ramsey holds in this model, while the atoms witness failure of CRT. The conjunction transfers to pure ZF by Pincus. Here are the required bounds. On sets admitting an injection from $\omega$, finite-arity Ramsey already holds in ZF. It therefore suffices to quantify universally over $X$ of injective cardinality at most $\aleph_0$ (supremum of the well-ordered cardinals that inject into $X$). This range contains every Dedekind-finite $X$. For each such $X$ the assertion, uniformly in finite $n$, is bounded in iterated power sets over $X$ and $\omega$: finite subsets, binary colorings and homogeneous subsets are all coded there. This is the restricted universal quantifier in Pincus's definition of injective boundability. Existence of a CRT counterexample is boundable. Both statements therefore transfer together. This also explains why a strict inclusion in a particular model is compatible with higher-arity nonimplications over ZF. [Blass's lemma as Lemma 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02215) · [Pincus transfer and injective boundability](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00605-021-01582-0)

**Canonization lemma.** Let $M$ be the countable generic linearly ordered $3$-uniform hypergraph, and $G$ its automorphism group. Every $G$-invariant equivalence relation $E$ on its increasing pairs has one of the four canonical forms. Use ultrahomogeneity and the extension property throughout: over a finite induced ordered hypergraph we can insert finitely many vertices in prescribed order cuts with arbitrary prescribed new triples.

If two disjoint pairs are related, every two pairs $p,p'$ sharing their first endpoint are related. Insert a fresh pair $q$ whose order pattern relative to each of $p,p'$ agrees with the order pattern of the second pair relative to the first in the original related configuration. This is possible since the common endpoint has the same role and the other two endpoints are on the same side of it. Each required cut below, between or above the endpoints has a common nonempty interval for both placements, and the order is dense without endpoints. Prescribe the triples on $p\cup q$ and on $p'\cup q$ from the original configuration. On the overlap they agree because the shared endpoint has the same role. By homogeneity $p\,E\,q\,E\,p'$. The identical argument works for pairs sharing their last endpoint. Changing one endpoint at a time connects all pairs: move the last endpoint past both given pairs, change the first, then change the last to the desired one. Hence $E$ is universal.

If distinct related pairs share their first endpoint, all pairs with any common first endpoint are related. For given $(x,y),(x,z)$ put an auxiliary $t$ above both $y,z$, or between $x$ and both, according to the order pattern of the original two outer endpoints. Set the two triples $\{x,y,t\},\{x,z,t\}$ to have the original triple's edge value. The common pair $(x,t)$ relates to both. The corresponding assertion holds for a common last endpoint. If two related pairs overlap across roles, their pattern is $(a,b),(b,c)$ with $a<b<c$, up to reversal of the relation. For two pairs with common first $x$, choose $t<x$ and use $(t,x)$ as a bridge, prescribing the two triples as just above. For two pairs with common last $x$, bridge with $(x,t)$ for $t>x$. This again makes $E$ universal. The remaining possibilities are equality, equality exactly on a common first endpoint, or equality exactly on a common last endpoint. This proves the lemma.

**A witness in the other direction.** Use the ordered $3$-Rado permutation model of Brot, Cao and Fernández-Bretón. The atoms $A$ are the union of countably many named copies of $M$, with independent component automorphisms and finite supports. Given an arbitrary pair coloring of $A$ in the model, take a copy disjoint from a finite support for the coloring. Equality of its color values is invariant on that copy under the full automorphism group. The lemma shows that the whole copy, with its given order, is an infinite canonical witness in the model. Thus CRT$(A)$ holds. Proposition 4.25 of the source proves that this same $A$ is $R_n$-finite for every $n\ge3$. This supplies the missing direction for all those arities in one model. Both local assertions transfer to pure ZF by the Jech–Sochor embedding: arbitrary colorings are represented by equivalence relations on sets of finite subsets, and witnesses and their orders are coded in bounded rank over the atoms and $\omega$.

**Interpretation and priority.** If the index in Question 8.1 is read as the number of colors on pairs, the previously known pair implication and the basic Fraenkel example already give the strict comparison. For the higher-arity classes actually denoted $R_n$-Fin in the cited source, the ordered triple-system lemma gives incomparability. Provisional significance **59/100** if correct and new. The model and its higher-arity negative property belong to the earlier authors. The proposed contribution is the canonization on that model and its bearing on the comparison.
