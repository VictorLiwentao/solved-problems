\section*{The question}
Amitayu Banerjee asked whether the infinite chain/antichain principle and the infinite Ramsey theorem for pairs hold in Bruce's finite partition model \cite{ban}. Here infinite means nonfinite; an infinite set need not contain a countable sequence of distinct elements. The model starts with a countably infinite set of atoms $A$ in an ambient universe with choice. A finite partition $P$ supports an object if every permutation in the group $G_P$ preserving each block setwise fixes the object. The model consists of the hereditarily supported objects \cite{bruce}.

\paragraph{Theorem.} In this model $\mathcal P(A)/\mathrm{Fin}$, ordered by inclusion modulo finite sets, is an infinite poset with no infinite chain and no infinite antichain. Coloring pairs by comparability witnesses the failure of the two-color infinite Ramsey theorem for pairs.

\paragraph{The quotient.} Every ambient subset $S$ of $A$ is internal, supported by the nonempty parts of $\{S,A\setminus S\}$. Finiteness for subsets of $A$ is absolute. Hence equality modulo finite symmetric difference and the ordering $[S]\le[T]$ iff $S\setminus T$ is finite have their usual ambient meanings. A support for a representative also supports its equivalence class. The family of all classes and its order are invariant under the whole group and belong to the model. For each positive integer $n$, an ambient partition of $A$ into $n$ infinite blocks yields $n$ distinct classes simultaneously in the model. Thus the quotient is infinite.

\paragraph{Every infinite internal subset contains both kinds of pairs.} Let $Y$ be an infinite internal subset of the quotient and let $P$ be a finite support for it. Only finitely many classes have a representative which is a union of blocks of $P$. Choose a class in $Y$ different from these and take a representative $S$. Some block $B\in P$ has both $S\cap B$ and $B\setminus S$ infinite. Otherwise the finitely many blocks could each be rounded to the empty or entire block with only finitely many points changed in total.

All infinite co-infinite subsets of $B$ are conjugate by ambient permutations of $B$, because $B$ is countably infinite in the ambient universe. Every such permutation extended by the identity off $B$ preserves $Y$. Consequently $Y$ contains all classes
\[
             [(S\setminus B)\cup T],
             \qquad T\subset B,\quad T,\ B\setminus T\ \text{infinite}.
\]
Partition $B$ into three infinite sets $B_1,B_2,B_3$. Substituting $T=B_1$ and $T=B_1\cup B_2$ gives distinct comparable classes. Taking $T=B_1$ and $T=B_2$ gives incomparable classes. These are actual members of $Y$; no internal enumeration is needed. Thus no infinite $Y$ is a chain or an antichain. The coloring of unordered pairs induced by the order is internal. Any infinite homogeneous set would be one of the two forbidden forms. \hfill$\square$

This answers the order and coloring parts of the same question with one witness. The basic Fraenkel model, which uses finite sets of atoms as supports, has a different Ramsey behavior; the paper posing the question records that distinction.
