\subsection{Partial enumeration of the dictionary chain}\label{partial-enumeration-of-the-dictionary-chain}

Open problem 2 in §5 of \emph{Flood and Harvest} concerns compression by successive abelian dictionaries. For a nonnegative integer vector \(x\) of fixed dimension \(n\geq1\), write \(|x|\) for its coordinate sum and \(s_q\) for the sum of its base-\(q\) digits, summed over coordinates. The source's Lemma D.7 identifies the compressible language at base \(q\) with
\[H_q=\{x:s_q(x)\leq\kappa\log_2(1+|x|)\}.\]
The bases start at an integer \(b\geq16\kappa^2\), \(\kappa\geq1\), and increase by squaring. The source orders vectors by raw length, breaking ties lexicographically.

\textbf{Theorem.} Every successor \(H_{q^2}\) has relative density zero in \(H_q\). In fact its count through raw radius \(R\) is smaller by a factor \(O(R^{-\delta})\) for some \(\delta>0\) depending on the fixed parameters. Every positive-lower-density partial core identifies the true index after finitely many observations. The generator can then attain the known-target guarantees while emitting at most finitely many points outside the target.

\textbf{Counting proof.} Write \(Q=q^2\). A column of base-\(Q\) digits of all coordinates is a symbol \(z=((a_i,b_i))_{i=1}^n\), with \(0\leq a_i,b_i<q\) and combined digits \(a_i+qb_i\). Its fine and coarse costs are \(f(z)=\sum_i(a_i+b_i)\) and \(g(z)=\sum_i(a_i+qb_i)\). Set \(B=\kappa\log_2 Q\). For \(v=f,g\) put \(M_v=\max\{H(p):\mathbb E_p v\leq B\}\) on the finite column alphabet, using natural-log Shannon entropy.

First \(M_g<M_f\). The uniform distribution has mean coarse cost \(n(Q-1)/2>B\) (use \(\kappa\leq\sqrt q/4\) and \(\log_2 q\leq q\) for \(q\geq16\)). The unique coarse maximizer is \(p(z)\propto\exp(-\lambda g(z))\) with \(\lambda>0\), since the tilted mean decreases continuously from its uniform value to zero; the relative-entropy inequality proves optimality. It has full support and positive weight on \(g>f\), so its mean fine cost is strictly below \(B\). Mixing it slightly with the uniform law strictly improves entropy and remains fine-feasible.

For \(Q^k\leq R<Q^{k+1}\), each coordinate of a vector of radius at most \(R\) has at most \(k+1\) columns. Coarse membership bounds the total cost by \(kB+O(1)\). A length-\(l\) word with empirical distribution \(p\) has at most \(\exp(lH(p))\) possibilities, and there are at most \((l+1)^{Q^n}\) types. These inequalities follow from the multinomial formula. Compactness of the probability simplex therefore bounds the coarse count by \(\exp(k(M_g+o(1)))\).

For the denominator choose a rational law \(p\) with mean fine cost strictly below \(B\) and entropy strictly above \(M_g\); mixing the fine maximizer with the zero column and rational approximation permits both. Fix an integer \(c\) with \(Q^c>2nQ\). Use \(k-c\) low columns with letter counts \((k-c)p(z)+O(1)\), and put a leading digit one in the first coordinate at position \(k-c\). These vectors have radius at least \(Q^{k-c}\) and strictly below \(Q^k\) for large \(k\). Their total fine cost is \((k-c)\mathbb E_p f+O(1)\), smaller than \(\kappa\log_2(1+|x|)\) for large \(k\). The multinomial formula and Stirling's estimate give \(\exp(k(H(p)+o(1)))\) distinct such vectors. The strict entropy gap proves the claimed power saving. Even if an ordered prefix stops inside a tie at radius \(R\), it contains this entire lower-bound family and its numerator is bounded by the full coarse ball.

\textbf{Learning consequence.} On each finite seen set return the deepest chain member containing it when such an index exists, with the first member as fallback. A core of positive lower density in \(H_q\) cannot be contained in \(H_{q^2}\). Once its first point outside \(H_{q^2}\) appears, the selected index is exactly the true one forever. A greedy least-unresolved-point race in the identified target gives lower density at least \(1/2\) in the full-prefix protocol, and also claims every never-enumerated point. A finite initial delay changes neither assertion (the greedy counting inequality loses only a fixed additive constant). Thus an exact-density-\(\alpha\) core permits coverage at least \(\max(1/2,1-\alpha)\) with finitely many nonvaluable outputs. If only set-valued breadth is wanted, output the identified language minus the seen set.
